Features

Every LED knows where it is.

01

Screen sync, HDR included

Backbloom captures your screen on the GPU and maps each edge of the picture to the LEDs behind it. HDR games and films are tone mapped, so the light matches what you see instead of washing out. It never touches the game itself.

02

Your room, mapped

Draw your strips where they really are, on your walls, ceiling and desk, around your monitor or TV. Pick a ready-made shape or drag the bends yourself. Each LED repeats the part of the screen it faces.

03

Music that moves the room

Nine music effects react to whatever is playing on your PC, or to a microphone. Give the ceiling the beat while the screen strip follows the picture.

04

A preset for every game

Gaming, Movie, Music and Cozy are ready to go, and you can add your own. Add your games and a preset takes over by itself while one runs. Presets can give strips other roles too: the whole screen on the ceiling in your racer, a calm color in your editor.

05

Set up in minutes, tuned for years

Stick a strip behind your screen, plug in the controller, and Backbloom's search finds WLED controllers on your network. Three short steps and the lights are on. White balance, calibration and a power limit wait under Setup when you want them.

Questions

I'm new to LED lights. What do I need?

A Windows 10 or 11 PC (Mac and Linux are planned) and something to light: usually an LED strip behind your screen with a small Wi-Fi controller (WLED) and a power supply, or smart bulbs and panels you already own. A ready-made controller needs no soldering. Not sure what fits your setup? Ask on GitHub.

Will it work with my Hue, Nanoleaf or Govee lights?

Philips Hue (through the Hue Bridge), Nanoleaf, LIFX, WiZ, Yeelight, Govee models with LAN Control, WLED and USB backlight kits like Skydimo work today, and you can mix brands in one setup. Older Govee models that only have Bluetooth are planned. Keyboard, mouse and PC case RGB are planned too.

Is it safe with anti-cheat? Can it get me banned?

Backbloom never reads game memory, injects into a game or needs a mod. It only watches the screen and listens to the sound, the same way recording and streaming apps do. That's also why it works with any game.

Will it lower my FPS or lag behind the game?

Screen capture and scaling run on your graphics card; the processor's share stays small: a few percent of one CPU core for a typical setup. With a quick Look, such as the Gaming preset's, the screen is read at least 60 times a second and LED strips keep up; smart bulbs update more slowly (Hue about 25 times a second), so they're best for setting the room's mood. If a game ever stutters, the app keeps a performance record on your PC that you can attach to a bug report.

Does it work with HDR, Netflix, a TV or my console?

It follows whatever your PC shows, HDR included, on a monitor or on a TV plugged into the PC. Some streaming apps block screen capture, so their videos can't be followed. A console only works if its picture goes through your PC.

Why not Hue Sync or my lights' own app?

Those drive one brand. Backbloom drives strips, bulbs and panels from many brands at once, lets you draw your strips where they really are in your room, and has presets that turn on by themselves when your game starts.

Is it free?

Yes. Backbloom is free and open source under the MIT License, and its code is on GitHub.

When are haptics and wind coming? Should I buy a shaker now?

They're planned after game events, with no dates yet. Follow the roadmap on GitHub to see when they land.

Found a bug or have a question?

Check the open issues first: if yours is there, give it a thumbs-up or add your details. Otherwise report a bug (in the app, Settings > About > Report a problem fills in your system details), or ask questions and share ideas in Discussions.
